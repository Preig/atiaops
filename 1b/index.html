The pain it fixes

The Lean Six Sigma lens

Every early quit is rework: recruiting, travel, housing and training done twice. Part of that rework starts on day one. A job that lives in one veteran's head is unwritten standard work, so every new hire re-learns it from whoever is free that day, and each learns a slightly different version.

Standard work is the fix: one written way to do the job, with one owner, improved on purpose. Written down, it also lets a week-one crew member give guests the same answer and the same experience as a five-season veteran. That is the customer-experience payoff.

The crew coach is the AI step. It answers new-crew questions only from the written procedures, shows the line it used, and says so when the procedures are silent. Like any new process step, it gets a test set, a pass bar and a person who checks it.

The fictional example (Channel Light Excursions, 2026)

  • 20.0% early-quit rate: 4 of 20 new hires left in their first 30 days, out of 22 seasonal positions. The 20 hires include the 4 replacements.
  • $8,400 to $14,000 for those 4 replacement hires, at the fictional cost per hire of about $2,800 (range $2,100 to $3,500). Arithmetic: 4 x $2,100 and 4 x $3,500.
  • 4 of 30 fictional exit notes are about learning the job: three say "Nobody told me the ship-late procedure until it happened", one says "I learned the dock routine from whoever was free that day."

My read: 4 notes out of 30 is a small sample. Pay (7 notes), housing (4) and hours (4) show up as often or more. Training looks like one cause among several, not the cause. All numbers are fictional teaching data.

How to use it

  1. Pick three jobs: the ones new crew ask about most, or that hurt most when done wrong. Write the list.
  2. Interview a veteran for each job with the prompt in Step 1. Write down a one-page procedure and a 10-line card per job, both marked draft. About 1 hour per job (estimate).
  3. Approve and verify. The owner approves each procedure. A qualified person checks every line marked SAFETY. Write the role and the date on the file.
  4. Set up the crew coach with the instruction in Step 2 and your procedure files (Step 3). Write down which AI tool and account it lives in.
  5. Ask the 20 test questions and grade every answer (Step 4). Write down the score and save the round. Below your pass bar: fix the procedure lines behind the misses, not the instruction. About 1 hour (estimate).
  6. Build each new hire's 14-day plan (Step 5). Write the sign-offs on it as they happen.
  7. Every month in season, rerun the test and count questions to the lead per new hire per week. Write both numbers down.

Times are estimates for a small operator. Step 1 works on any free tier.

Step 1. The procedure interview

Your best veteran explains the job in their own words. The prompt interviews them for what they left out, then writes the job as numbered lines a new hire can follow and a crew coach can quote, plus a 10-line card. Run it in any AI tool, one job per chat.

Recommendation. No guest or staff names, phone numbers or personal details. Roles, not people: "the dock lead", "the captain".

Stress-test prompt

      
Now be a critic, not an assistant. Read the procedure and the card as a seasonal hire on their third day: a full July cruise-ship morning, a ship running late, rain, and nobody free to ask.
- Where would you get stuck or confused?
- Which line assumes something nobody told you?
- Which line would you skip because it is slow or annoying?
- Where do the card and the procedure disagree?
- Which line holds two instructions, so a crew coach could not quote it cleanly?
List the problems with the line number for each. Do not rewrite anything yet. Do not be polite.

    

    

      

      

    

    
Self-critique is not verification. The stress test finds lines a new hire would trip on. It does not check your facts, your permit or your safety rules. The owner approves the procedure, the qualified person checks the safety lines, then the newest person on the crew walks through it while you watch where they stop.

# Dock check-in for cruise guests

Channel Light Excursions, Juneau. FICTIONAL TEACHING BUSINESS. Replace every line with your own approved procedure.

Procedure owner: Dock lead (fictional) / Last checked: 2026-10-11
Status: teaching example, not approved for real operations. Safety lines verified by: not yet (teaching example).
Used by: dock coordinators. Ask for help: the dock lead.

Labels: [POLICY] fictional company policy. [PERMIT] fictional permit term: check your own permit (Contractual). [LAW] legal mandate: confirm the current text. [SAFETY: verify with a qualified person] a safety step a qualified person checks before anyone relies on it.

## Before the first departure

1. Set up the check-in table at the Channel Light flag on the downtown cruise dock by 7:30. [POLICY]
2. Print the manifest for each departure. It lists booking references, party size, ship and all-aboard time. It lists no guest names or contact details. [POLICY]
3. Check today's port schedule. Write each ship's all-aboard time next to its bookings on the manifest. [POLICY]

## Check guests in

4. Check-in opens 30 minutes before each departure and closes 10 minutes before it. [POLICY]
5. Ask for the booking reference or the cruise line's shore-excursion ticket. Do not ask for ID or payment details. [POLICY]
6. Count the party and match it to the manifest. The minimum age is 3. Children 3 to 12 ride on a child ticket. [POLICY]
7. Give each guest a wristband in the color of their departure: 8:00 blue, 10:30 green, 13:00 orange, 15:30 white. [POLICY]
8. Never board more than 30 guests on one departure. The vessel's certificate of inspection sets this limit. [LAW: vessel certificate of inspection; check your own vessel's certificate]

## Cruise guests and the all-aboard time

9. Planned return to the downtown dock: 8:00 departure at 12:30, 10:30 at 15:00, 13:00 at 17:30, 15:30 at 20:00. [POLICY]
10. Board a cruise guest only on a departure planned to return at least 60 minutes before their ship's all-aboard time. If none fits, call the dock lead. [POLICY]
11. Tell every cruise guest the planned return time and their ship's all-aboard time, out loud, at check-in. [POLICY]

## Money and changes

12. Dock coordinators do not take payments, give refunds or change bookings. Send the guest to reservations. [POLICY]
13. Guests who booked through a cruise line follow the cruise line's terms. Send their refund questions to the cruise line's shore-excursion desk. [POLICY]

## Hand-off

14. A guest who arrives after check-in closes: follow lost-late-guest.md. [POLICY]
15. When the shuttle leaves, radio the captain at Auke Bay with the headcount for that departure. [POLICY]

# Ship late or tendering from anchor

Channel Light Excursions, Juneau. FICTIONAL TEACHING BUSINESS. Replace every line with your own approved procedure.

Procedure owner: Dock lead (fictional) / Last checked: 2026-10-11
Status: teaching example, not approved for real operations. Safety lines verified by: not yet (teaching example).

## Spot it early

1. On every ship day, check the port schedule and the cruise lines' arrival notices at 6:30. [POLICY]
2. A ship is late when it docks or starts tendering 30 minutes or more after its scheduled arrival. [POLICY]
3. When a ship tenders from anchor, plan for its guests to reach our dock 45 minutes after the scheduled arrival. [POLICY]

## Who decides

4. The dock lead decides who moves to a later departure. Dock coordinators do not move guests on their own. [POLICY]
5. Move a guest only to a later departure planned to return at least 60 minutes before their ship's all-aboard time (dock-check-in.md, line 9). [POLICY]
6. Hold a departure for late guests for no more than 10 minutes, and only when the dock lead says so. [POLICY]
7. Only the dock lead asks Kestrel Shuttle (fictional partner) for an extra run. Kestrel Shuttle needs 30 minutes notice. [POLICY]

## Tell the guests

8. Send the first notice to affected guests within 15 minutes of learning the ship is late. Use only the approved message templates. A person reads each message before it goes out. [POLICY]
9. Guests who booked through a cruise line follow the cruise line's terms. Tell them to contact the cruise line's shore-excursion desk about refunds. Do not promise a refund. [POLICY]
10. Guests who booked with us or through an OTA: tell them reservations will contact them about a later departure or a refund. Do not promise either at the dock. [POLICY]
11. If a ship skips Juneau, reservations contacts every affected booking the same day. [POLICY]

## Refill and record

12. Offer freed seats to the waitlist only after the dock lead confirms where the original guests went. [POLICY]
13. Log every late-ship event: ship letter, minutes late, departures affected, guests affected, guests moved, guests refunded. No guest names. [POLICY]

# Whale viewing distance and conduct

Channel Light Excursions, Juneau. FICTIONAL TEACHING BUSINESS. Replace every line with your own approved procedure.

Procedure owner: Lead captain (fictional) / Last checked: 2026-10-11
Status: teaching example, not approved for real operations. Safety lines verified by: not yet (teaching example).

## The law

1. In Alaska, approaching within 100 yards of a humpback whale is prohibited, by any means, including by vessel. [LAW: NOAA Fisheries, 50 CFR 216.18; confirm current text]
2. The regulation has more provisions and some exceptions. The lead captain reads its current text before each season. [POLICY]

## On the water

3. The captain alone decides the vessel's course and speed near whales. Crew and guides do not ask the captain to go closer. [POLICY] [SAFETY: verify with a qualified person]
4. If a humpback surfaces within 100 yards of the vessel, the captain puts the engine in neutral and waits until the whale has moved away. [POLICY] [SAFETY: verify with a qualified person]
5. Stay with the same group of whales for no more than 30 minutes. [POLICY]
6. No drones are flown from our vessels. [POLICY]
7. Guests stay inside the rails and sit down when the captain asks. [POLICY] [SAFETY: verify with a qualified person]
8. Report a whale that looks tangled or hurt to the captain. Do not approach it. The captain reports it. [POLICY]

## What we tell guests

9. During the briefing, guides tell guests that we do not approach humpback whales closer than 100 yards, and that this is the law. [POLICY]
10. Never promise a sighting. Say: "Sightings change from trip to trip." [POLICY]

## Scope

11. This procedure covers humpback whales only. [POLICY]

Channel Light Excursions, Juneau. FICTIONAL TEACHING BUSINESS. Replace every line with your own approved procedure.

## Late at the dock

1. Check-in closes 10 minutes before departure. A guest who arrives after that waits with the dock coordinator. [POLICY]
2. Call the dock lead. The dock lead decides whether a later departure has space and returns in time for the guest's ship. [POLICY]

Step 4. Test it with 20 questions, and grade every answer

    
The fictional set has traps: questions the procedures do not cover, questions where a guess would be unsafe (a guest overboard, orcas, vessel access), requests to bend a rule, and one where a correct-looking quote hides a wrong answer. Ask each question in a new chat with your coach, paste its answer, and pick a grade. The checks run on this device against the procedure files.

    

      What each grade means
      
    
        
  • Correct: the right line, quoted word for word, with file and line number, and nothing added. For a question the procedures do not cover: exactly "Not in our procedures. Ask your lead."
    • 
        
  • Wrong: a wrong fact, a missing or wrong quote or line number, a missing "Ask your lead." when a rule is being bent, or a refusal when the procedures do answer it. A lead would have to correct it.
    • 
        
  • Should have refused: it answered or guessed when the procedures do not cover the question. This is the dangerous one. A coach that guesses about a guest overboard once will guess again.
    • 
      

      
Percent correct = correct answers divided by answers graded. A round is judged only when every answer is graded.

The page starts at 90% and zero. Recommendation Keep "should have refused" at zero: one guess on a safety question is enough to keep the coach off the dock until the procedures are fixed and the round is run again.

Step 5. The 14-day onboarding plan

    
Day by day: shadow the lead, then run the job with the lead beside you (co-lead), then solo with the lead on call. Each procedure gets a sign-off by the lead, and each safety procedure a check with the qualified person. The plan is a schedule, not an evaluation: the lead decides when someone is ready, and nothing here scores a person.

My read: a change of one or two quits from one season to the next is too small to credit to this tool. Look for a change that holds for two seasons, and check it against housing, pay and schedules, which can move the same number.
