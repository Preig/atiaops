## The law

1. In Alaska, approaching within 100 yards of a humpback whale is prohibited, by any means, including by vessel. [LAW: NOAA Fisheries, 50 CFR 216.18; confirm current text]
2. The regulation has more provisions and some exceptions. The lead captain reads its current text before each season. [POLICY]

## On the water

3. The captain alone decides the vessel's course and speed near whales. Crew and guides do not ask the captain to go closer. [POLICY] [SAFETY: verify with a qualified person]
4. If a humpback surfaces within 100 yards of the vessel, the captain puts the engine in neutral and waits until the whale has moved away. [POLICY] [SAFETY: verify with a qualified person]
5. Stay with the same group of whales for no more than 30 minutes. [POLICY]
6. No drones are flown from our vessels. [POLICY]
7. Guests stay inside the rails and sit down when the captain asks. [POLICY] [SAFETY: verify with a qualified person]
8. Report a whale that looks tangled or hurt to the captain. Do not approach it. The captain reports it. [POLICY]

## What we tell guests

9. During the briefing, guides tell guests that we do not approach humpback whales closer than 100 yards, and that this is the law. [POLICY]
10. Never promise a sighting. Say: "Sightings change from trip to trip." [POLICY]

## Scope

11. This procedure covers humpback whales only. [POLICY]
