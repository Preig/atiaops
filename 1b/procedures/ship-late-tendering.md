## Spot it early

1. On every ship day, check the port schedule and the cruise lines' arrival notices at 6:30. [POLICY]
2. A ship is late when it docks or starts tendering 30 minutes or more after its scheduled arrival. [POLICY]
3. When a ship tenders from anchor, plan for its guests to reach our dock 45 minutes after the scheduled arrival. [POLICY]

## Who decides

4. The dock lead decides who moves to a later departure. Dock coordinators do not move guests on their own. [POLICY]
5. Move a guest only to a later departure planned to return at least 60 minutes before their ship's all-aboard time (dock-check-in.md, line 9). [POLICY]
6. Hold a departure for late guests for no more than 10 minutes, and only when the dock lead says so. [POLICY]
7. Only the dock lead asks Kestrel Shuttle (fictional partner) for an extra run. Kestrel Shuttle needs 30 minutes notice. [POLICY]

## Tell the guests

8. Send the first notice to affected guests within 15 minutes of learning the ship is late. Use only the approved message templates. A person reads each message before it goes out. [POLICY]
9. Guests who booked through a cruise line follow the cruise line's terms. Tell them to contact the cruise line's shore-excursion desk about refunds. Do not promise a refund. [POLICY]
10. Guests who booked with us or through an OTA: tell them reservations will contact them about a later departure or a refund. Do not promise either at the dock. [POLICY]
11. If a ship skips Juneau, reservations contacts every affected booking the same day. [POLICY]

## Refill and record

12. Offer freed seats to the waitlist only after the dock lead confirms where the original guests went. [POLICY]
13. Log every late-ship event: ship letter, minutes late, departures affected, guests affected, guests moved, guests refunded. No guest names. [POLICY]
