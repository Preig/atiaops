The method: DMAIC, not the news

Tools change every month. A defined problem, a baseline and a control plan do not. My read: most AI disappointment starts at Improve, with a tool bought before the problem was defined or measured.

  1. DDefine

    One pain, one number. Pick from the four numbers.

  2. MMeasure

    A baseline, as a range: the Season Leak Audit.

  3. AAnalyze

    Causes before tools: Pareto, journey FMEA, slot-use map.

  4. IImprove

    Now AI comes in: drafts, a crew coach, a re-planner, an optimizer.

  5. CControl

    A person approves, a test set runs monthly, the number is tracked season over season.

Pain 1 Staff turnoverrecruiting and retraining cost

In Alaska: 54.2% of accommodation workers and 35.2% across visitor-related industries were nonresidents in 2024 (Alaska DOLWD). My read: a large share of every crew is new each spring, and the know-how leaves each fall.

Lean lens: every early quit is rework: recruiting, travel, housing and training done twice for one position.

  • Basic · 1.a OPS Rehire before you recruit Pareto of season-end notes, rehire letters, an honest job post with checkable facts. An afternoon · free tier
  • Intermediate · 1.b OPS CX Standard work and crew coach Procedures out of one head, and a coach that answers new crew only from them. A day to set up, then an hour a procedure · paid tier
  • Advanced · 1.c OPS Season staffing model Your attrition curve turned into a hiring plan by date and channel, with cost ranges. 1 to 3 weeks · analyst or developer

Pain 2 Transit bottleneckslost operational revenue

In Alaska: The Denali Park Road was cut at Pretty Rocks for five seasons (2021 to 2026); two state ferries were out of summer 2026 revenue service; about 1.97 million cruise visitors were expected in 2026, through the same docks and roads. No statewide figure for revenue lost to transit disruption exists: measure your own with 2.c.

Lean lens: arrival-time variation is an input you do not control. Detect early, respond with standard work, price the losses by cause.

  • Basic · 2.a CX OPS Journey FMEA and playbook Rank failure modes on the way to your dock; standard responses and guest messages for the top five. An afternoon · free tier
  • Intermediate · 2.b OPS Arrival watch and backfill Watch the port schedule, alerts and bookings; drafts and waitlist offers go to an approval queue. About a week · no-code automation · monthly fee
  • Advanced · 2.c OPS Re-planner and loss ledger Re-plan a delayed day under seats and all-aboard limits; lost revenue by cause, season over season. 3 to 6 weeks · developer

Pain 3 Capacity capsgrowth stalls at the cap

In Alaska: Juneau limits cruise arrivals to 16,000 a day from 2026 (12,000 on Saturdays); Mendenhall Glacier commercial tours are capped at 517,650 visitors a season and sold out mid-season in 2023; Glacier Bay admits two cruise ships a day. Most operators statewide are not under a hard cap: check which constraint binds you.

Lean lens: Theory of Constraints. Identify the constraint that actually binds, exploit it, subordinate everything else to it, then grow where it does not bind.

  • Basic · 3.a OPS Fill every capped slot Slot-use map by month and weekday; a pricing and packaging memo you decide on. An afternoon · free tier
  • Intermediate · 3.b PRODUCT Off-cap product lab Light days in next season's ship schedule, product concepts that avoid the cap, a scoring grid to kill most of them. About a week · paid tier
  • Advanced · 3.c OPS Revenue-per-slot optimizer Product mix and price options per day type, tested across ranges; an evidence pack of actual use. 3 to 6 weeks · analyst or developer

Your first 30 days: DMAIC in four sittings

One sitting a week. Off season is the right time: the data is fresh and nobody is on the dock.

  1. Week 1, Define and measure: run the Season Leak Audit with last season's numbers and write down the four baselines below.
  2. Week 2, Analyze the crew: the Pareto of season-end notes in 1.a. Rehire letters go out before any ad.
  3. Week 3, Analyze the road: the journey FMEA and the message pack for your top five failure modes. Decide your rebook and refund rules before May. Policy choice
  4. Week 4, Analyze the cap: the slot-use map. Then choose one intermediate rung from the map for spring. Bring your numbers to a no-cost Alaska SBDC advising session at aksbdc.org. Recommendation
