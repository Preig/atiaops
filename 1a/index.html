The pain it fixes

Every early quit is rework: recruiting, travel, housing and training done twice. Every returning crew member is a hire you do not make and a week of training you do not repeat. The Lean move is to Pareto the reasons people leave and stay, fix the vital few, and then ask the right people back before you advertise. Write the job post last, with the hard parts in it, so the people who say yes know what they are saying yes to.

For 2027, 20 of this season's crew finished in good standing and are eligible to return. My read: each yes from that list saves one hire, about $2,800 by the fictional estimate, and the person already knows the dock, the boat and the ship-late procedure.

Fictional teaching business: Channel Light Excursions, Juneau, season May 1 to September 30, 2026. The cost per hire is a fictional build-up (ads, sponsor fees, travel, housing setup, hiring time, training, trainer time, ramp-up); put your own in the Season Leak Audit.

How to use it

  1. Collect season-end notes before the crew scatters: three written questions, no names. "What would make you come back? What nearly made you leave? What should we fix first?" Add role and yes, maybe or no to returning. Write them into the template.
  2. Run the Pareto below and read the notes under the top themes. Write down the top two themes and the note IDs behind them.
  3. Copy the Pareto prompt into Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini. Check every quote against the notes. Write down the two fixes you decide on, who owns each, and the date.
  4. Build the rehire message with those fixes. Write down your rule for who is asked back, then send the same message to everyone it covers. Log each yes, maybe and no.
  5. Build the honest job post for the positions still open. Run the word finder on the AI's draft and write down every [FILL] you still owe.
  6. Give every new hire the first-14-days checklist. Write the check-in dates in the schedule.
  7. Count Numbers 1 and 2 when the season starts and after day 30. Write them next to this season's.

            4 · First-14-days checklist
            
    
            My read: an early quit usually starts as a small problem nobody asked about. A short checklist with three check-ins, a buddy and written procedures turns the first two weeks into standard work. In the fictional notes, 3 of 30 said "Nobody told me the ship-late procedure until it happened."
            
    
            
      Download the checklist (.md)
      Download as a tracker (.csv)
    
            
    
            
      What is in it
      
              
        
            1. Before day 1: housing confirmed in writing; first two weeks of schedule with days off; pay, pay dates and overtime in writing; a named buddy; sponsor terms checked for J-1 or H-2B staff; new-hire paperwork from your payroll provider's list.

            2. Day 1: walk-through; role safety briefing by a qualified person; wildlife rules (100-yard humpback rule); the ship-late procedure, read together; who to ask.

            How you will know it worked
            
    
              
      
            • Number 1 · Rehire rate (want up) = staff who return this season ÷ staff eligible to return from last season (finished in good standing). Count it once, when the season starts.
              • 
      
            • Number 2 · Early-quit rate (want down) = new hires who leave in their first 30 days ÷ new hires who started. Count it once every new hire has passed day 30.
              • 
    
            
    
            No targets: set them after a season of your own data. Compare season to season. Small crews make big swings: with 20 new hires, one early quit moves the rate 5 points, so read the direction over two or three seasons before you credit a fix.

            Before you use it
            
    
            AI drafts. You approve. Every prompt marks its answer DRAFT - PENDING OWNER REVIEW. A person edits and sends every message; nothing is sent from this page.
            
    
              
      
            • Legal mandate Federal anti-discrimination law (Title VII, ADA, ADEA) applies to rehire and hiring decisions, whatever tool helps make them. Confirm current text with a qualified person. This page never scores, ranks or flags individual people, and the prompts tell the AI not to.
              • 
      
            • Policy choice Who is asked back. Write the rule down (for example: finished the season in good standing) and send the same message to everyone it covers.
              • 
      
            • Policy choice The pay range and any returning bonus are the owner's decisions. Contractual Once a pay rate or bonus is offered in writing, treat it as a term of that offer: check what you sent before you change it.
              • 
      
            • Legal mandate Alaska overtime law applies to the hours you post and schedule: AS 23.10.060 (daily and weekly overtime, with exemptions in AS 23.10.055). Confirm with Alaska DOLWD Wage and Hour. Recommendation Say in the post how overtime is paid.
              • 
      
            • Contractual For J-1 or H-2B staff, obligations vary by sponsor and program. Check your sponsor agreement and current DOL and State Department rules before you promise dates, housing or pay to sponsored staff.
              • 
      
            • Recommendation De-identify notes before you paste them: no names, contact details, health details or immigration status. The page catches contact details, not everything. Cost of ignoring it: personal data in an AI chat history you do not control.
              • 
      
            • Recommendation List the hard parts in the post. Cost of ignoring it, in my read: new hires who discover them in week one, inside the early-quit window.
