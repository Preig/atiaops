The pain it fixes

A capped product sells only as many slots as the permit allows, so the cap sets the pace of the whole product. Theory of Constraints says: identify the constraint, exploit it (get full use out of it), subordinate everything else to it, elevate it if you can, then repeat. Under a cap, an empty slot on a light day and a no-show on a capped day are both lost for good. A regulatory cap is not yours to elevate, so getting full use out of it is the job. But first check which constraint actually binds: guests can be turned away by the cap, or by seats and departure times, and only the second is yours to change.

The fictional example: Channel Light Excursions, Juneau (FICTIONAL TEACHING BUSINESS)

  • 79.1% of the glacier allocation used: 12,096 of 15,300 slots (100 guest visits a day, May 1 to September 30, 2026).
  • 66 days at the cap (5 or fewer slots left). July ran at 99%. The empty slots sat in May (56% used), September (62%) and on Saturdays (66%).
  • 1,936 combo requests turned away (estimate): only 206 at the glacier cap, and 1,730 because no departure had seats at a time that fit the guest's ship.
  • $115.67 revenue per allocated slot (combo net revenue $1,769,688 over 15,300 slots). That is Number 4.

Teaching point: identify which constraint actually binds, day by day, before you exploit it. In the example, 1,423 of the 1,730 seats-or-timing turn-aways fell on the 66 days the cap was reached, when only 65 glacier slots were left: on those days both constraints bind, and an extra seat sells the uncapped trip (Whale Only), not another glacier combo. Only 307 fell on the 87 days below the cap. That is where checking the departure schedule against the ships' in-port windows, a constraint the owner can elevate, wins back combos.

My read On capped days the lever is the value of each slot, not more seats; on days below the cap it is filling empty slots and fitting departures to the ships' hours. Load the example below: the map splits the 1,730 by whether the cap was reached that day.

How to use it

  1. Export one row per day from your booking system or permit log: date, allocation, used. Add no-shows and turned-away requests if you track them. Write down where the export came from.
  2. Paste or upload it below and fix any skipped rows. Write down the season's allocation used % and days at the cap.
  3. Read the map. Find where slots sat empty and where the cap bound. Write down the two lightest months or weekdays.
  4. Check which constraint binds. Compare requests turned away at the cap with those turned away for seats or timing. Write down your first move.
  5. Copy the memo prompt into Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini (a free tier works). Read the options against your summary. Write down the one or two you will test and who decides.
  6. Work through the permit check with your permit in hand. Write down each answer with its clause.
  7. Count Number 4 at season end and compare it with this season.

Times are estimates for an operator who can export daily counts. Without an export, typing a season of daily counts into a spreadsheet adds an estimated 1 to 2 hours.

    Why these defaults

    Value of one slot. $115.67 is the fictional revenue per allocated slot (Number 4): net revenue of the capped product divided by every allocated slot, used or not. It is lower than the net revenue from a guest who actually used a slot, so it values an empty slot cautiously. Use your own Number 4, or your average net per guest on the capped product.

    At the cap. A day with 5 or fewer slots left counts as at the cap, because a leftover smaller than a typical party rarely sells. Change it to match your party sizes. Policy choice

    Fill share. Not every empty slot can be sold. 30% is the same planning assumption the Season Leak Audit uses; it is not a forecast. Write down your own reason for the number you pick.

            Before you use it
            
    
            AI drafts. You approve. The memo is a list of options marked DRAFT - PENDING OWNER REVIEW. Price, deposit and schedule decisions stay with the owner.
            
    
              
      
            • Contractual Permit terms. How use is counted, actual-use reporting, renewal and transfers are set by your permit or agreement (USFS, NPS, city). Check your permit. This kit makes no claim about how use affects future allocation.
              • 
      
            • Legal mandate Seats are set by the vessel. If you add seats or departures to fix a seats-or-timing constraint, the passenger limit comes from each vessel's Coast Guard certificate of inspection. Vessel-specific: confirm the current certificate.
              • 
      
            • Contractual Channel contracts. Cruise line and OTA agreements can set rate parity, allotments and cancellation terms. Check your contracts before any price, deposit or cutoff change.
              • 
      
            • Legal mandate Lodge packages show the total price. The FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees, 16 CFR Part 464, covers short-term lodging and live-event tickets, so a package that includes a lodge night shows the total price, with every fee the guest cannot avoid. Tours sold alone are not covered by that rule. Confirm current text.
              • 
      
            • Legal mandate No fake reviews. The FTC Consumer Review Rule, 16 CFR Part 465, bans fake or AI-invented reviews and testimonials. Light-day marketing uses none. Confirm current text.
              • 
      
            • Recommendation No automatic price changes. A person decides every price. Reason: a price set by a tool can break a channel contract or a published total price. The cost of ignoring it: guest trust and contract disputes.
              • 
      
            • Policy choice Deposits, waitlists and cutoffs on capped days are the owner's decisions. Write them down and publish them before you sell under them.
              • 
      
            • Recommendation Counts only. This tool needs dates and counts, never guest data. Reason: guest data in a spreadsheet or AI chat is a breach risk. Alaska's Personal Information Protection Act, AS 45.48, sets breach-notification duties for certain personal information you hold. Legal mandate
