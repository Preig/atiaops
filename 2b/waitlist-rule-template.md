# Waitlist rule template

From the ATIA Operations Kit, tool 2b Arrival watch and backfill: https://preig.github.io/atiaops/2b/
A starting checklist, not legal advice. Teaching examples are fictional (Channel Light Excursions, Juneau). Checked 11 Oct 2026.

A freed seat is worth something only if someone can take it before the boat leaves. Decide this rule once, before the season, write it into your 2a disruption policy sheet, and the rebook board, the drafts and your staff all follow the same order.

Each line says what kind of obligation it is:

- **Legal mandate**: a law or regulation. Cited; confirm the current text.
- **Contractual**: a contract, permit or agreement you signed. Check it.
- **Policy choice**: your decision. You could decide it differently.
- **Recommendation**: advice, with the reason and the cost of ignoring it.

---

## 1. Who can join the waitlist (Policy choice)

- [ ] Guests turned away because a departure was full.
- [ ] Guests turned away because the day's glacier allocation was used up.
- [ ] Which channels: direct, OTA, cruise line desk. Write each one: ______________________
- [ ] Cruise-line guests: whether the shore excursion desk may take a waitlist offer, and how. **Contractual:** check your cruise line contract. ______________________

Fictional example: direct and OTA guests turned away for a full departure or a used-up allocation; cruise-line guests only through the desk.

## 2. The order (Policy choice)

Pick one and write it down:

- [ ] **First come, first served**, by the time the party joined.
- [ ] **Best fit**: the largest party that fits the freed seats first, then by the time joined. Fills more seats; can feel unfair to a small party that waited longer.
- [ ] Other: ______________________

And:

- [ ] **Parties are never split.** (Fictional example: yes.)
- [ ] Repeat or returning guests get priority: yes / no. If yes, say how you check it without keeping extra personal data.

## 3. The offer (Policy choice)

- Minimum time between the offer and the departure, in minutes: ______ (fictional example: 45). Less than this, the guest cannot get to the dock.
- How long an offer stays open before it goes to the next party, in minutes: ______
- How many offers go out at once for the same seats: one at a time / all who fit (first to reply wins). Write which: ______
- The price: the same as a normal booking, shown in full. No fake urgency ("only today", "last chance") unless it is literally true. **Recommendation:** a waitlist offer that reads like a sales trick costs more trust than the seat is worth.

## 4. Limits the offer never breaks

- **Seats:** never more guests on a departure than the vessel's Coast Guard certificate of inspection allows. **Legal mandate** (vessel-specific; check your certificate).
- **Glacier or park allocation:** a refilled combo seat uses one of the day's guest visits; a guest moved within the same day does not. **Contractual:** check your permit's terms on counts and reporting.
- **All-aboard buffer** for cruise guests: the offer only goes to guests who are back at the dock at least ______ minutes before their ship's all-aboard (fictional example: 60). **Policy choice.**
- **Walk-off time** from the ship to your meeting point: ______ minutes docked, ______ minutes at anchor (fictional example: 30 and 60). **Policy choice.**

## 5. Who approves and sends (Policy choice)

- Every waitlist offer is a draft until one named person approves it: ______________________ (a role, for example Duty lead).
- No automation sends an offer. A person sends it from the booking system or email and records sent_by and sent_at in the approval sheet.

## 6. Records (Recommendation)

- Keep: waitlist ID, the time the party joined, party size, product, ship (or "independent"), and what happened (offered, accepted, declined, expired).
- Keep contact details in your booking system, not in the waitlist sheet or any AI tool.
- Delete waitlist contact details after the season.
- If you ever hold personal information as Alaska law defines it (for example a name with a payment card or ID number), the Alaska Personal Information Protection Act, AS 45.48, sets breach-notification duties. **Legal mandate**; confirm the current text.

## 7. Measure it

- Seats refilled after disruption = freed seats a waitlist or walk-up guest took / freed seats. Per event, added up per season.
- Number 3, disruption recovery rate = guests on disrupted bookings who were rebooked or whose seat was refilled / all guests on disrupted bookings.
- Compare season to season. A season has a few dozen events, so month-to-month swings are mostly noise.
