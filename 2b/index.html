Arrival watch and backfill

The earlier you know a ship is late or a departure is off, the more seats you can save. Practice the rebook on a fictional day, then set up a no-code watch that drafts the messages into an approval sheet. A person approves and sends every one.

  • Time 1 hour to practice; 1 to 2 days to set up one watch (estimate)
  • Cost a Zapier or Make plan, roughly $20 to $30 a month (estimate; check current plans)
  • Who duty lead, plus whoever builds your automations
  • Feeds Number 3, disruption recovery rate

The pain it fixes

Detection is a control. In the journey FMEA (2a), a late ship you notice at the dock scores detection 6 to 8; the same ship flagged by a watched port schedule an hour earlier scores 3 or 4. That hour is what lets you move guests to an earlier departure and offer the freed seats to your waitlist before the boat leaves. Once the alert is in, the rebook is a small, rule-bound puzzle: seats, the all-aboard buffer, travel times. A tool can lay it out in a second. A person still decides and sends.

How to use it

  1. Run the four fictional days on the rebook board. On each, change "We knew at" by an hour and watch the moves and offers change. Write down what an hour of warning is worth on your busiest day.
  2. Set your rules under "Rules for the board": your buffer, walk-off times, notice and waitlist rule. Write them into your 2a policy sheet.
  3. Practice the approval queue: read each draft, fix the [FILL] items, approve with your initials. Write down how many minutes the queue took.
  4. Pick one watch from the recipes (the port-schedule sheet is the easiest) and build it in Zapier or Make with the fictional rows first. Write down the date it passed the test.
  5. Decide your waitlist rule with the template and write it into the policy sheet.
  6. Each month in season, count freed seats and refilled seats, and write down Number 3.

1. Rebook board simulator

Pick a fictional day and an event. The board loads that day's manifest (from the fictional bookings and port schedule, built into this page so it works offline), works out which bookings the event hurts, which other departures still fit (seats, the walk from the ship, the all-aboard buffer, time to reach the guest), which waitlist guests can take the freed seats, and drafts the messages into an approval queue. It sends nothing.

The manifest holds booking references, counts, products and ships. No names, emails or phone numbers. Everything stays in this browser.

2. Approval queue

Every draft from the board lands here as template text, not AI text. Each one starts with DRAFT - PENDING OWNER REVIEW and has [FILL] where only a person knows the answer. Cruise-line bookings go to the ship's shore excursion desk, one message per ship. Nothing on this page sends anything. Approving marks the draft as ready for a person to send from your booking system or email.

Approvals here are practice. They reset when you change the day or the event. In real use, the queue is a shared sheet (template in Downloads) and the person who sends a message writes sent_by and sent_at.

3. Watch recipes for Zapier and Make

Untested in live accounts. These recipes are written from each product's documented building blocks and checked for logic, not run end to end in a real Zapier or Make account. Step names change. Build with the fictional rows first and check every step against the product's current help before any real booking touches it.

The four watches

  • Port-schedule sheet change (start here). Your team keeps a shared sheet of ship calls; when someone changes an arrival time, berth or status, the watch fires. Free of extra fees beyond your plan.
  • National Weather Service alerts through the public api.weather.gov (no key; the service asks callers to identify themselves with a User-Agent header). Find your own forecast zone on weather.gov, including the marine zone for your waters. The alert only tells the captain; the captain makes the call, then the duty lead marks the hold and the drafts follow.
  • Booking-system webhook. New, changed or cancelled bookings update the day manifest; a same-day cancellation frees seats for the waitlist. Check whether your booking system offers webhooks or a Zapier or Make app.
  • Flight status, paid option only. A paid flight-data API, for guests who chose to give you a flight number. It costs per lookup; check the provider's price and terms first. Skip it until the free watches work.

Zapier

One Zap per watch, plus one that drafts after the captain records a hold. One approval sheet, a staff alert. Polling and webhook triggers, Google Sheets lookups, optional AI by Zapier or your AI provider's app, Gmail or SMS to staff only.

You draft the first notice about a disruption for [BUSINESS NAME]. A person approves every message before anyone sends it. You never send anything, and you do not decide rebooking options.

EVENT (from our watch sheet):
[EVENT: what changed, and the time we first knew]

AFFECTED BOOKINGS (IDs and counts only; no names or contact details):
[ROWS: booking_ref, channel, guests, booked departure, ship]

OUR APPROVED POLICIES (use only these; a line marked [FILL] is not decided, so do not guess it):
[POLICY SHEET FROM 2A]

OUR APPROVED FIRST-NOTICE TEXTS (start from these):
[FIRST NOTICES FROM THE 2A MESSAGE PACK]

TASK
Draft the first notice for this event:
- Cruise-line bookings: one message per ship, to that ship's shore excursion desk, listing the booking references and party sizes.
- OTA bookings: one message per booking, written for the OTA's messaging tool.
- Direct bookings: one message per booking, to the guest.
Each first notice says what we know, what we do not know yet, and when we will update. Under 80 words each.

Rules:
- Use only the event, the rows, the policies and the approved texts. Do not add times, prices, refunds, credits, compensation or waiting promises that are not written above.
- Do not offer or suggest a new departure. The duty lead sends the options after working out seats and the all-aboard buffer.
- Do not promise weather, wildlife, or that a ship, flight, shuttle or boat will wait. Do not make or hint at a weather or safety call; the captain makes it.
- Use [GUEST FIRST NAME] where a name goes. Names and contact details are merged in our booking system, never here.
- Where something is missing, write [FILL: what is missing].
- Start each message with three lines: DRAFT - PENDING OWNER REVIEW, then TO: the recipient's role (for example Ship B shore excursion desk), then ABOUT: the booking references.
- After the last message, list every [FILL] under QUESTIONS FOR THE DUTY LEAD.

A .md file is plain text: open it in Notepad, TextEdit, Word or Google Docs. Running cost is an estimate: a polling watch that checks every 15 minutes for 14 hours a day runs 4 x 14 = 56 times a day, about 8,600 times over a 153-day season. Check how your plan counts runs that find nothing.

4. Waitlist rule template Policy choice

A freed seat is worth something only if someone can take it before the boat leaves. Decide the rule once, write it in your 2a policy sheet, and the board, the drafts and your staff all follow the same order.

  • Who can join: guests turned away for a full departure or a used-up glacier allocation; which channels.
  • Order: first come, first served, or best fit. Parties are never split.
  • How long an offer stays open, and how many offers go out at once.
  • Minimum notice before a departure for an offer to make sense.
  • Glacier allocation: a refilled combo seat uses a visit Contractual (check your permit).
  • Cruise-line guests: whether and how the desk takes a waitlist offer Contractual (check your contract).
  • Price: the same as a normal booking, shown in full. No fake urgency.
  • Records: waitlist IDs, party size and the time they joined. Delete contact details after the season.

How you will know it worked

  • Number 3, disruption recovery rate: guests on disrupted bookings who were rebooked or whose seat was refilled, divided by all guests on disrupted bookings. Per season, by cause. Fictional 2026: 191 of 545 = 35.0%.
  • Seats refilled after disruption: freed seats that a waitlist or walk-up guest took, divided by freed seats. Count it per event, add it up per season. Fictional 2026 has 64 seats refilled; it did not record freed seats, so start recording both.
  • Minutes from first alert to first message: the watch should push this down. Record both times on every event.

A season has a few dozen events and guests on one event share an outcome, so month-to-month swings are mostly noise. Compare season to season, and by cause. On the fictional 2026 log, resampling the 38 events gives a rough 95% range of 28% to 42% around the 35.0% recovery rate. (Arithmetic on the fictional data; see 2a.)

Before you use it

    
AI drafts. You approve. The board and the recipes create drafts and approval rows only. No step sends a message, books, refunds or cancels anything. Every draft carries DRAFT - PENDING OWNER REVIEW until a person approves it.

    
    
      
  • Recommendation Weather and vessel safety calls stay with the captain or another qualified person. A weather alert starts a heads-up to the captain, never a guest message. Reason: an automation that drafts "trip on" or "trip off" before the captain decides puts pressure on a safety call.
    • 
      
  • Legal mandate Passenger limits come from each vessel's Coast Guard certificate of inspection, and they are vessel-specific. A move or a refilled seat never goes past it. Check your certificate.
    • 
      
  • Legal mandate Personal information. If your sheets ever hold personal information as Alaska law defines it (for example a name with a payment card or ID number), the Alaska Personal Information Protection Act, AS 45.48, sets breach-notification duties. Confirm the current text. Keep guest details in your booking system; the sheets in these recipes hold IDs, counts and times.
    • 
      
  • Contractual Cruise-line guests: who contacts them, which refund applies and whether a waitlist offer is allowed come from your cruise line contract. Check it.
    • 
      
  • Contractual OTA guests: messaging outside the OTA and refund terms come from your OTA supplier agreement. Check it.
    • 
      
  • Contractual Tools and data: check the terms of Zapier or Make, your AI provider, Google Workspace, your booking platform and any flight-data API before you connect them.
    • 
      
  • Contractual Glacier allocation: refilled combo seats use the day's visits. Check your permit's terms on counts and reporting.
    • 
      
  • Policy choice The buffer, notice time, walk-off times, waitlist rule, offer window and who approves. The board shows the effect of each; you decide them.
