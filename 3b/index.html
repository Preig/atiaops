3.b Off-cap product lab

Find next season's light days, have AI draft product concepts that use no capped slot, score them with a kill-or-test rule you set before you score, and draft a light-day offer calendar with honest total prices. AI drafts; you decide and publish.

The pain it fixes

Theory of Constraints, step 5: repeat. Once the cap gets full use (3.a) and the rest of the operation serves it, go back to step 1 for the parts of the business the cap does not touch. On light days, and for products that use no capped slot, the constraint is demand: there is no product those guests want yet. Product development works on that constraint. Step 5 also carries a warning: do not let inertia, selling only the capped product, become the constraint. Step 4, elevate, is the land manager's decision for a regulatory cap, made through the permit process, so this lab does not plan on more allocation.

The fictional example: Channel Light Excursions, Juneau (FICTIONAL TEACHING BUSINESS)

  • 66 days at the glacier cap in 2026, while May (56% used), September (62%) and Saturdays (66%) left slots empty.
  • Whale Only ($159 adult, fictional) uses no glacier slot. It is the base for products that grow off the cap.
  • Tern Point Lodge (fictional partner) sells packages for independent travelers in May, early June and September: the light months.

How to use it

  1. Set the light-day rule (daily limit and thresholds) and read the calendar. Use the fictional 2027 schedule or paste your port's schedule. Write down the light days by month.
  2. List what the cap does not touch: boats, crew, partners, products, guest groups. Write them into the concept prompt fields.
  3. Copy the 5-concept prompt into Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini. Write the concepts worth a look into the concept card builder.
  4. Set the kill-or-test rule, then score each concept with your team. Write down the decision and the reason for each.
  5. Copy the offer calendar prompt for the "test" concepts. Write down the offers you approve, with total prices.
  6. Set up the weekly refresh (optional). Write down who reviews the drafts each week.
  7. Count the share of bookings on light days at season end, and Number 4.

Times are estimates for a small operator with one capped product and one or two partners.

How a day is classified

Port load is passengers in port that day divided by the daily limit you enter. The fictional schedule uses 16,000, and 12,000 on Saturdays; these are teaching values for an invented schedule. Use the limit that applies at your port.

Your last-season use is your capped product's allocation used % on the same weekday in the same month last season (for 2027 dates, the fictional 2026 glacier use).

Heavy if either signal is high: port load at or above the heavy threshold, or your use at or above yours. Light only if both are low: port load below the light threshold and your use below yours (or no use data). Medium is everything else. The rule is deliberately strict about "light", so an offer never lands on a day the cap is likely to bind.

The thresholds are a policy choice. Write down why you picked them, and keep the same rule all season so the light-day share stays comparable.

  • Llight: the target days
  • Mmedium
  • Hheavy: the cap may bind

            How you will know it worked
            
    
              
      
            • Share of bookings on light days: bookings on light days divided by all bookings, with the same light-day rule all season. Compare season to season.
              • 
      
            • Off-cap net revenue: net revenue from products that use no capped slot. This is the growth.
              • 
      
            • Number 4, revenue per allocated slot: net revenue of the capped product divided by allocated slots. My read It should not fall. If it does, an off-cap product is pulling guests off the capped product on capped days, which breaks step 3 (subordinate: the cap comes first).
              • 
      
            • Each test: the kill result you wrote on the card, checked when the test ends.
              • 
    
            
    
            Monthly counts of light-day bookings are small, so month-to-month swings are mostly noise. Judge the share season to season, and look at passengers in port alongside it.

            Before you use it
            
    
            AI drafts. You approve. Every prompt asks for DRAFT - PENDING OWNER REVIEW and [FILL] gaps instead of guesses. The owner sets prices, decides kill or test and publishes every offer.
            
    
              
      
            • Legal mandate Lodge packages: total price. FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees, 16 CFR Part 464, covers short-term lodging and live-event tickets. A package with a lodge night shows the total price. Tours sold alone are not covered by that rule. Confirm current text.
              • 
      
            • Legal mandate No fake reviews. FTC Consumer Review Rule, 16 CFR Part 465: no fake or AI-invented reviews or testimonials in any marketing copy. Confirm current text.
              • 
      
            • Legal mandate Vessel capacity. Passenger limits come from each vessel's Coast Guard certificate of inspection. A new departure never sells above it. Vessel-specific: confirm the current certificate.
              • 
      
            • Legal mandate Whale viewing distance. Approaching humpback whales in Alaska within 100 yards is prohibited (NOAA Fisheries, 50 CFR 216.18). No product promises closer viewing or a sighting. Confirm current text.
              • 
      
            • Contractual Partners. A package with Tern Point Lodge (fictional) or any real partner needs written rates, room blocks, cancellation terms and who refunds what. Check your partner agreement.
              • 
      
            • Contractual Channels. A locals' or light-day price can conflict with rate-parity clauses in OTA or cruise line contracts. Check your contracts before you publish a lower price.
              • 
      
            • Contractual Permits. A new product that uses public land, a park or a permitted site may need its own permit or authorization. Check your permit and ask the land manager.
              • 
      
            • Recommendation No automatic price changes or publishing. The weekly recipe drafts only. Reason: a tool that publishes or prices on its own can break a contract or a published total price before anyone sees it.
              • 
      
            • Policy choice The light-day rule, the weights and the kill-or-test rule are the owner's. Set them before scoring and keep them all season.
              • 
      
            • Recommendation Schedules and counts only. No guest or staff data in the prompts, the sheet or the automation. Alaska's Personal Information Protection Act, AS 45.48, sets breach-notification duties for certain personal information you hold. Legal mandate
